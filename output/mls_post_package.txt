===== TITLE =====

How to Read LDL, HDL and Triglycerides

===== HOOK =====

Your lipid panel has several numbers. Which ones actually matter?

===== DESCRIPTION =====

A lipid panel includes several measurements, and each one provides different information.

1. LDL CHOLESTEROL
LDL is one of the main atherogenic cholesterol measures used in cardiovascular risk assessment. Its interpretation depends on overall cardiovascular risk and clinical context.

2. HDL CHOLESTEROL
HDL is one component of the lipid profile. HDL alone should not be used to determine overall cardiovascular risk.

3. TRIGLYCERIDES
Triglycerides measure a type of fat in the blood. Very high levels have clinical significance and are associated with pancreatitis risk.

4. TOTAL CHOLESTEROL
Total cholesterol represents cholesterol carried in several lipoprotein particles. It should be interpreted together with the other lipid measurements.

5. THE COMPLETE PATTERN
One lipid value alone does not determine overall cardiovascular risk. The complete lipid profile should be interpreted together with overall cardiovascular risk and clinical context.

===== TAKEAWAY =====

Read the complete lipid pattern, not one number alone.
