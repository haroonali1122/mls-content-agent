TITLE:
How to Read LDL, HDL and Triglycerides

===== HOOK =====

Your lipid panel has several numbers. Which ones actually matter?

===== CORE PROBLEM =====

People often see LDL, HDL, triglycerides and total cholesterol on one report but do not know how to interpret the complete pattern.

===== CONTENT =====

LDL CHOLESTEROL

LDL is one of the main atherogenic cholesterol measures used in cardiovascular risk assessment.

Its interpretation depends on overall cardiovascular risk and clinical context.


HDL CHOLESTEROL

HDL is one component of the lipid profile.

HDL alone should not be used to determine overall cardiovascular risk.


TRIGLYCERIDES

Triglycerides measure a type of fat in the blood.

Very high triglyceride levels have clinical significance and are associated with pancreatitis risk.


TOTAL CHOLESTEROL

Total cholesterol represents cholesterol carried in several lipoprotein particles.

It should be interpreted together with the other lipid measurements.


THE COMPLETE PATTERN

Do not judge cardiovascular risk from one lipid value alone.

Interpret the complete lipid profile together with the person's overall cardiovascular risk and clinical context.

===== ACCURACY RULES =====

Do not use one universal lipid target for every person.

Separate laboratory reference ranges from treatment targets.

Do not diagnose cardiovascular disease from a lipid panel alone.

Do not describe HDL alone as complete cardiovascular protection.
